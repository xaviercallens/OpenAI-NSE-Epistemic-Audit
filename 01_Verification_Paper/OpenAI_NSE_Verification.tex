\subsection{\texorpdfstring{The Gevrey Bypass: Satisfying $C^\infty$ with Physically Impossible Gradients}{The Gevrey Bypass: Satisfying C-infinity with Physically Impossible Gradients}}

The Millennium Prize requires a perfectly smooth ($C^\infty$) external force. Were the force
required to be real-analytic ($C^\omega$), it would be completely rigid: a function zero outside a
localized region must be identically zero everywhere by the identity theorem. The formalization
bypasses this rigidity by employing Gevrey-class cutoffs of the form
$\chi(q) \sim \exp(-1/q^2)$.

By classical analysis, the function $\exp(-1/q^k)$ has an exact theoretical asymptotic Gevrey index of $s = 1 + 1/k$.
For $k = 2$, the true theoretical Gevrey index is $s = 1.5$ (symbolic finite-sample fits with $N \le 15$ yield an empirical pre-asymptotic slope $\approx 2.22$).
This is the precise mathematical signature distinguishing Gevrey functions from analytic ones:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Gevrey-1.5} \;\subset\; C^\infty \setminus C^\omega.
\end{equation}
All flat limits $\lim_{q \to 0^+} d^N [q^{-A} \exp(-1/q^2)] / dq^N = 0$ hold for every $N$ and
$A$---confirmed by SymPy---so the function possesses infinitely many derivatives and legally
satisfies the Clay $C^\infty$ requirement.

However, this mathematical legality conceals a physical impossibility. The physical vulnerability of Gevrey cutoffs lies in their \emph{non-analyticity}. In true incompressible fluids, the pressure Poisson equation ($\nabla^2 p = -\rho \nabla \cdot (u \cdot \nabla u)$) requires global, instantaneous pressure communication. Gevrey compact cutoffs allow the artificial creation of compactly supported fluid ``bubbles'' that do not analytically communicate pressure with the surrounding domain, mathematically shielding the blow-up profile from global boundary relaxation. The formalization exploits the exact boundary between $C^\infty$ and $C^\omega$---mathematically impeccable, physically unrealizable.

\subsection{Lighthill Acoustic Radiation Catastrophe}

When an unsteady fluid flow accelerates in a physical medium, fluid compressibility produces acoustic quadrupole radiation. According to Sir James Lighthill's acoustic analogy and eighth-power law~\cite{Lighthill1952,Proudman1952}, the total acoustic power radiated by a localized turbulent eddy of characteristic velocity $U$ and length scale $L$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{ac}} = C_{\text{quad}} \frac{\rho_0}{c_s^5} U^8 L^2,
  \label{eq:lighthill}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\text{quad}} \sim \mathcal{O}(1)$ is the aerodynamic quadrupole efficiency tensor factor and $\rho_0$ is fluid mass density. In the OpenAI self-similar vortex scaling, $U(\tau) = u_0 \tau^{-1/2-h}$ and $L(\tau) = \ell_r(\tau) = \ell_0 \tau^{1/2}$. Substituting these scalings into Eq.~\eqref{eq:lighthill} reveals the exact asymptotic power law:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{ac}}(\tau) \sim \frac{\rho_0}{c_s^5} \left(u_0 \tau^{-1/2-h}\right)^8 \left(\ell_0 \tau^{1/2}\right)^2 = \frac{\rho_0 u_0^8 \ell_0^2}{c_s^5} \tau^{-4 - 8h + 1} = \frac{\rho_0 u_0^8 \ell_0^2}{c_s^5} \tau^{-3 - 8h}.
\end{equation}
For the parameter $h = 1/200 = 0.005$, the instantaneous radiated acoustic power diverges with exponent:
\begin{equation}
  P_{\text{ac}}(\tau) \propto \tau^{-3.0400} \;\to\; \infty \quad (\text{as } \tau \to 0).
\end{equation}
Even more destructive is the intensive acoustic power \emph{density} $p_{\text{ac}} = P_{\text{ac}} / V_{\text{core}}$, where the cylindrical core volume is $V_{\text{core}} = \pi \ell_r^2 \ell_z = \pi \ell_0^3 \tau^{1/2 + 1/2 + 1/2 - h} = \pi \ell_0^3 \tau^{3/2 - h}$:
\begin{equation}
  p_{\text{ac}}(\tau) = \frac{P_{\text{ac}}}{V_{\text{core}}} \propto \tau^{-3 - 8h - (3/2 - h)} = \tau^{-9/2 - 7h} = \tau^{-4.5350} \;\to\; \infty.
\end{equation}
Integrating $P_{\text{ac}}(\tau)$ over time proves that the cumulative radiated acoustic energy diverges:
\begin{equation}
  E_{\text{ac}}(\tau) = \int_\tau^{\tau_0} P_{\text{ac}}(\tau')\,d\tau' \sim \tau^{-2.0400} \;\to\; \infty.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Laboratory Observations: Reconnection and Dissipation Saturation}

Real laboratory fluid experiments decisively contradict the singular collapsing-vortex scenario. Landmark experiments by Kleckner and Irvine~\cite{KlecknerIrvine2013}, utilizing 3D-printed hydrofoils and high-speed laser scanning tomography in water, demonstrated that when isolated vortex filaments come into close proximity, they do not undergo self-similar collapse. Instead, vortex filaments undergo topological \emph{vortex reconnection}: the core lines collide, pinch off, and rearrange into unknotted vortex rings, shedding helicity into acoustic waves and small-scale turbulence.

Furthermore, extensive direct numerical simulations (DNS) and high-Reynolds-number experimental measurements by Schumacher, Sreenivasan, and coworkers~\cite{Schumacher2014,Donzis2008} reveal that extreme dissipative events in physical turbulence are inherently self-limiting. Although enstrophy can momentarily exceed mean values by several orders of magnitude, viscous diffusion at the Kolmogorov microscale $\eta = (\nu^3/\varepsilon)^{1/4}$ continuously diffuses high-vorticity patches. The local enstrophy density saturates at a finite molecular bound, in perfect accordance with the Beale-Kato-Majda regularizing mechanism~\cite{BKM1984}.
