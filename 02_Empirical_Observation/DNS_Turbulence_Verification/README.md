## The physical question, stated correctly

The constructed Navier–Stokes core has enstrophy $\Omega\sim\tau^{-0.515}$ and local energy density
$e_{\text{local}}\sim\tau^{-1.010}$, while its total energy goes to zero. Its core Reynolds number stays of order
one, so its scales are diffusive: the flow leaves the regime the incompressible model describes (Mach, Knudsen and
Eckert numbers all of order one at $\ell_*=\nu/c_s$, about 0.7 nm in water) a few **picoseconds** before the
singularity — Mach 0.3 about 6.7 ps before, the core radius at molecular size about 90 fs before. That is a
statement about which model the theorem is about.

Kolmogorov's cascade picture does not apply to this core: it is a manufactured, forced, $\mathrm{Re}\approx1$
collapse, not turbulence. And "viscosity caps enstrophy" is not what the model does — the construction's enstrophy
does diverge, with viscosity included. What stops a *real* fluid, if anything, has been tested separately
(paper §9): kinetic theory does not arrest a driven core; on this route compressibility and heat do not either
before $\ell_*$; in water cavitation comes first.

## Scripts

- `analyze_dns_enstrophy.py`: enstrophy, dissipation rate and Kolmogorov scale of a supplied 3D velocity array;
  demo on synthetic random data.
- `run_ml_sgs_falsification.py` (filename kept for history): Gaussian-filter illustration of subgrid smoothing.
- `../../scripts/generate_falsification_graphs.py` (filename kept for history): plots the analytic scalings of the
  construction against the Mach and local-vorticity bounds.
