\section{Introduction}
A common point of confusion in interdisciplinary fluid dynamics, raised insightfully by \texttt{u/brokebeany}, is the distinction between a fluid being \emph{divergence-free} and a fluid being \emph{incompressible}.

\section{The Mathematical vs. Physical Gap}
In the OpenAI Lean 4 proof, the constructed vector field $u(x,t)$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \nabla \cdot u = 0 \quad \text{for all } t \in [0, 1)
\end{equation}
Mathematically, this means the PDE is perfectly satisfied. However, in physics, the equation $\nabla \cdot u = 0$ is derived from the continuity equation $\partial_t \rho + \nabla \cdot (\rho u) = 0$ by assuming that density $\rho$ is strictly constant.

This assumption is an asymptotic limit that only holds when the Mach number $Ma = |u|/c_s \ll 1$. When the fluid in the OpenAI singularity breaches $Ma = 0.3$ (at roughly $\tau = 67$ femtoseconds), physical density variations scale as $\Delta \rho / \rho \sim Ma^2 \sim 0.1$. 

Therefore, while the \emph{vector field} remains mathematically divergence-free, the \emph{physical fluid} it is supposed to represent becomes compressible. The equation stops matching reality.
