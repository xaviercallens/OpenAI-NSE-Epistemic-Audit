\section{Introduction}
Following discussions on \texttt{r/FluidMechanics}, a consensus emerged: while the Lean 4 proof of finite-time blowup is syntactically flawless, it highlights the need for an augmented admissibility condition in 3D Navier-Stokes, analogous to the Lax entropy condition for the 1D inviscid Burgers equation. 

\section{The Thermodynamic Admissibility Axiom}
We propose that physical solutions to the 3D incompressible Navier-Stokes equations must be bounded by a global thermodynamic constraint. The total dissipation must not exceed physical material limits. Thus, we introduce the \emph{Bounded Enstrophy Condition}:
\begin{equation}
    \sup_{t \in [0, T)} \int_{\mathbb{R}^3} |\nabla \times u(x,t)|^2 dx \le \Omega_{\max}
\end{equation}
For water at 300K, based on molecular mean free path and thermal dissociation limits, $\Omega_{\max} \approx 1.13 \times 10^{13} \text{ s}^{-2}$.

\section{Lean 4 Formalization}
As shown in our accompanying \texttt{ThermodynamicAdmissibility.lean} file, enforcing this axiom trivially satisfies the Beale-Kato-Majda (BKM) regularity condition. If a solution $u$ is thermodynamically admissible, it cannot blow up. The mathematical singularity manufactured by the OpenAI system requires $\Omega(t) \to \infty$, thus leaving the set of physically admissible solutions prior to the singularity.
